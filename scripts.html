Too lazy to make thumbnails

54s · Creator talking to camera over screen recording

Permission-to-be-lazy hook, then the time-or-money problem, then the tool that fixes both, then a proof number and the offer.

Want this one? Reply with S1.

  1. 0-3sHook

    If you're too lazy to make thumbnails for your own videos, it's fine actually. This makes them in 90 seconds.

    How to shoot it Creator to camera, mid-sentence energy, no intro. Text on screen mirrors the first five words. Cut on the word 'actually' to a screen recording.

  2. 3-10sStakes

    In 2026 the thumbnail decides if anyone clicks, that's non-negotiable. But a good thumbnail is either two hours in Photoshop or $50 to a designer.

    How to shoot it Two-sided framing on screen: left 'Time' with a clock, right 'Money' with a price. Keep the creator on camera in a corner PIP.

  3. 10-14sTurn

    But now there's 1of10's thumbnail generator. It solves both.

    How to shoot it Logo lockup for one second, then straight back to the product. Do not linger on the logo.

  4. 14-32sDemo

    You connect your channel and it learns how your thumbnails look. Type the video title. It gives you a set of thumbnails built on what actually gets clicked on YouTube. Don't like the text? Change it with AI. Don't like the background? Change it.

    How to shoot it Screen recording, 3 to 4 seconds per step: the title being typed, the grid of thumbnails appearing, then one AI edit.

  5. 32-40sProof

    Look, this is six thumbnails for one video in under two minutes. That would normally be an afternoon in Photoshop or fifty bucks a pop.

    How to shoot it Hold the finished thumbnails on screen for the whole beat. Put the number in big type: ‘6 thumbnails, 2 minutes’.

  6. 40-54sOffer and close

    And right now you can try it for $1. If you’re posting videos without testing thumbnails, you’re leaving views on the table.

    How to shoot it Back to camera for the close. End card for 2 seconds: ‘Try it for $1’ and 1of10.com.

    How to shoot it Screen recording of typing the title. Show that it’s one action. Zoom in on the click.

  3. 12-22sDemo

    And in about 90 seconds you have six thumbnails in your channel's style, ready to go.

  6. 38-44sOffer and close

“I didn’t” interview

39s · Interview, off-camera question, creator answers

The “I didn’t” answer is the hook. The interview lets a real user do the selling, and the features come out as memories, not a list.

Want this one? Reply with S3.

  1. 0-4sHook

    How did you make thumbnails before 1of10? I didn't. I used the frame YouTube picked.

    How to shoot it Off-camera interviewer, subject mid-shot, natural light. Hard cut on 'I didn't'. Subtitle the question.

  2. 4-12sBefore state

    I'd spend two hours on Canva and it still looked like every other thumbnail in my niche.

    How to shoot it B-roll of the old way: the frame YouTube auto-picked, or a messy Canva thumbnail. Keep the interview audio running.

  3. 12-22sThe moment it changed

    1of10 let me type the title, and boom, six thumbnails that looked like my channel but better.

    How to shoot it Screen recording over the interview audio at 'boom'. Show the result appearing.

  4. 22-32sResult in the user's words

    My click-through went up on the first video. It learned my style. The text, the faces, the contrast. That's what made people click.

    How to shoot it Cut between subject and the feature they name. Each feature name gets a one-second on-screen label.

  5. 32-39sClose

    At the end of the day it's the first thing I open after I film. Title in, thumbnail out.

    How to shoot it Subject to camera for the last line. End card: ‘Try it for $1’ and 1of10.com.

The big number

Want this one? Reply with S4.

  1. 0-4sHook

    We're at two million views this year, and the thumbnails on every one of those came out of 1of10.

    How to shoot it You on camera with your YouTube Studio analytics behind you or picture-in-picture. Number in big type. Use your real number.

  2. 4-12sHow they use it

    I type the title and let it decide the concept. It comes back with the ones I'd have picked myself, faster.

    How to shoot it Screen recording, hands-off feel. Cursor does one click and then waits.

S5

Street interview

39s · Street interview, then phone demo to the subject

Find a creator who makes their own thumbnails and show them the app on your phone. Their reaction is the proof.

Want this one? Reply with S5.

  1. 0-6sApproach and question

    Hey, looking good. What do you do for a living?

    How to shoot it Handheld, street, natural sound. The interviewer is warm, not salesy. Subject should be a real creator or coach.

  2. 6-16sFind the pain

    I make YouTube videos, coaching stuff. And you make your own thumbnails? Yeah man. How long does one take? An hour, two if I hate it.

    How to shoot it Let the subject say the hours out loud. That number is the hook for the second half. Subtitle everything.

  3. 16-30sShow them the app

    Try this. What's that? Let me show you. Type your video title. Ninety seconds. There's six thumbnails, in your style.

    How to shoot it Phone held between them, screen visible to camera. Cut to a clean screen capture for the actual demo, then back to the subject's face for the reaction.

  4. 30-39sPrice and close

    And you can try it for $1. 1of10.com.

    How to shoot it Get the subject’s reaction line if you can (‘wait, that’s it?’). End card with 1of10.com.

S6

“Hey you, come here”

53s · Creator physically leads the viewer somewhere, whispered secret

Direct address plus “come here” is a physical hook: you move, the camera follows. The “secret” framing makes the features feel like insider info.

Want this one? Reply with S6.

  1. 0-5sHook

    Hey you, you! You want to know how I get clicks on every thumbnail? Come here.

    How to shoot it Point straight at the lens, beckon, and walk; the camera follows handheld through a door or around a corner to your desk. A snap or whistle on ‘Hey you’, then a quick ‘shh’ before the reveal. Can’t walk? Lean into the lens, lower your voice and add a whisper sound effect.

  2. 5-14sBust the expensive assumption

    You don't have to hire a designer or learn Photoshop to get a thumbnail that gets clicked.

    How to shoot it Arrive at the desk, sit, spin to the screen. Show the 'expensive' option crossed out on screen (an editor invoice, a Photoshop icon).

  3. 14-30sName the goods

    With 1of10 you get a thumbnail generator trained on millions of thumbnails that already got clicked, it learns your channel's style, and you can start free.

    How to shoot it Screen recording with each name on screen as it's said. Keep pace brisk, one second per name.

  4. 30-42sRange

    From the title to the thumbnail to the AI edit, everything is in one place. Plus it gets better the more you upload, because it learns your style.

    How to shoot it Two-shot montage: the beginning of the workflow and the end result. Whisper delivery continues.

  5. 42-53sNo strings, close

    The best part? You can try it for $1 at 1of10.com.

    How to shoot it Back to the creator, normal voice for the last line, wink or point at lens as a bookend to the opening. End card.
